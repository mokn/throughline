This market will resolve according to the calendar month (ET) of OpenAI's IPO date, as defined below.

The "Pricing Prospectus" is the first final prospectus for the IPO filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) through its EDGAR system under Rule 424(b) under the Securities Act of 1933. A later prospectus or prospectus supplement, including one for an increase in the size of the offering or a change in the offering price, does not replace it.

OpenAI's IPO is considered completed only when all three of the following have occurred: (i) the SEC has declared OpenAI's registration statement for the offering effective; (ii) a Qualifying Exchange has approved OpenAI's common equity securities for listing; and (iii) the offering has been priced, as shown by the Pricing Prospectus stating the number of shares offered and the offering price. A "Qualifying Exchange" is the New York Stock Exchange, NYSE American, the Nasdaq Stock Market or Cboe BZX Exchange, and no other. The IPO date is the date stated on the cover of the Pricing Prospectus, read as a date in ET, provided that the events in (i) and (ii) occurred on or before that date. A registration statement declared effective, or a listing approval, without pricing does not count, and neither does a preliminary prospectus published at the start of the marketing of the offering.

Only the IPO date determines the resolving month; the date of the first trade in OpenAI's shares in secondary trading on an exchange does not. An IPO whose IPO date falls on the last day of a month resolves to that month even if its shares first trade on an exchange in the following month. A completed IPO counts even if the offering is later withdrawn or the shares do not begin trading.

Only a traditional IPO qualifies: a priced, underwritten offering of newly issued common equity securities of OpenAI, or of American depositary shares representing them, sold to the public for cash under a registration statement on Form S-1 or Form F-1. An offering in which selling stockholders also sell shares qualifies if it includes newly issued shares sold by OpenAI. It must be the first public offering and listing of OpenAI's common equity securities on any securities exchange. An IPO by a parent or holding company formed to hold all or substantially all of OpenAI's business counts as an IPO by OpenAI; an IPO by a subsidiary or separated business of OpenAI does not.

Direct listings, mergers with special purpose acquisition companies (SPACs), reverse mergers, private placements, offerings limited to institutional or accredited investors, and any listing without an underwritten, SEC-registered offering of new shares are not IPOs. Media reports, announcements of an intention to go public, and the public filing of a registration statement do not count.

If OpenAI's IPO is priced but the Pricing Prospectus has not been published on EDGAR by the end of the pricing day, this market will wait for it until 11:59 PM ET on the second business day after the end of the month in which the IPO was priced.

If OpenAI's IPO date falls on or before December 31, 2026, this market will resolve to "December 2026 or earlier".

If OpenAI's IPO date does not fall on or before December 31, 2027, or if before then OpenAI is acquired by or merged into another entity (other than a holding company formed for the purpose of its IPO), its equity securities become listed or publicly traded other than through a qualifying IPO, or it otherwise permanently ceases to be able to complete an IPO, this market will resolve to "No IPO before 2028".

The primary resolution source will be the SEC's EDGAR system, for the Pricing Prospectus, the registration statement and the notice that the SEC has declared it effective, and the Qualifying Exchange's certification approving OpenAI's securities for listing, together with the Qualifying Exchange's official notice of the listing. OpenAI's official announcements and a consensus of credible reporting may be used only if those sources have not published the relevant filing or notice within two business days after the event is otherwise public.
