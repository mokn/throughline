This market will resolve to "Yes" if any Group Company has a Credit Event after this market is created and on or before December 31, 2027, 11:59 PM ET. Otherwise, it will resolve to "No". A Credit Event counts even if later reversed.
Definitions
"Listed Company": Crusoe Inc., or its successor by name change, reincorporation or new holding company, but not any other company that Controls it.
"Group Company": The Listed Company or any company it Controls, including financing vehicles even if their lenders have no claim on the Listed Company, except one with under $10 million of liabilities and no Obligations to outside parties.
"Controls": Directly or indirectly owns more than 50% of the voting shares of, consolidates, or otherwise controls under SEC Regulation S-X Rule 1-02(g).
"Debt": Borrowing of any ranking, including hybrid debt such as AT1 bonds, and guarantees of others' borrowing.
"Obligations": Money a Group Company owes under Debt; any lease; a contract with a financing vehicle, controlled or not, that funds assets it uses (such as off-balance-sheet data centers or chips); any other written commercial contract with at least $10 million committed; or a final judgment or arbitration award on these. Not ordinary trade payables, taxes, fines, penalties, wages, benefits or amounts owed between Group Companies.
"Distressed": A Group Company is Distressed if, in the 30 days before a deal or waiver is announced: (a) it, the Listed Company or the affected Debt was rated CCC+ or lower (Caa1 or lower at Moody's) by an agency in Credit Event item 7; (b) a US-dollar bond of it or the Listed Company had an end-of-day yield at least 10 percentage points above the nearest-maturity US Treasury on 10 trading days in a row (skipping days without trades), per FINRA TRACE or credible reporting; or (c) it said the deal is needed to avoid default or bankruptcy.
Credit Events
1. Bankruptcy: A Group Company files under US Chapter 7 or 11, or becomes subject to a similar insolvency proceeding anywhere (such as administration, voluntary administration, liquidation or a suspension of payments) or to a scheme of arrangement, restructuring plan or similar court process (such as a Dutch WHOA) that cuts what creditors are owed. A creditors' petition counts once granted, dated at filing if granted within 30 days, otherwise at the grant. A solvent wind-down paying all creditors doesn't count.
2. Missed payment: One or more Group Companies fail to pay at least $10 million of Obligations in total, each amount still unpaid at the later of 30 days after its due date and the end of any grace period (plus 30 days if an outage or cyberattack on payment systems caused the failure). Amounts disputed in a court or arbitration case filed before the due date don't count; market-rent disputes don't qualify.
3. Covenant breach: A Group Company breaches a covenant on at least $10 million of Debt; it admits the breach or a court finds it; and lenders demand immediate repayment, it isn't fixed or waived within 30 days of public disclosure, or it is waived while the Group Company is Distressed. A forbearance or standstill isn't a waiver.
4. Distressed restructuring: While a Group Company is Distressed, at least $10 million of its Obligations is exchanged, tendered or repurchased, in or out of court, for cash, new Debt or stock worth less than face value (listed stock at its closing price when the deal takes effect; unlisted stock at the price in the deal or credible reporting); has payments cut or delayed; or switches to paying interest in more Debt (PIK). Also counted, at any price: an exchange, tender or repurchase by a Distressed Group Company that uses a holder vote or consent to strip or weaken the covenants, security or payment priority of the remaining Obligations.
5. Refusal to pay: A Group Company states in a press release, filing or written notice to creditors that becomes public that it won't pay at least $10 million of Obligations, due or past due, or that its board approved a bankruptcy filing. Saying it may be unable to pay, is reviewing options or seeking a standstill, or will use a grace period doesn't count, nor do amounts disputed under item 2.
6. Write-down: A government or regulator cancels or writes down a Group Company's Obligations, or its Debt is written down or converted to stock under its own terms because a financial-condition trigger (such as a capital, solvency or liquidity test) is hit or a regulator or court finds it failing or likely to fail. Other conversions (scheduled, stock-price-triggered, holder-elected or company-induced) don't count here; an induced conversion while Distressed counts under item 4.
7. Default rating: Any of these on a Group Company or its Debt: S&P SD, D or R; Moody's D-PD, /LD or a public statement that a deal is a default; Fitch RD or D; Morningstar DBRS SD or D; KBRA D; or AM Best d, e, f, E or F. Other agencies don't count.
Not Credit Events on their own:
Open-market bond or loan purchases not tied to a vote, consent or amendment affecting remaining holders
Relief a landlord, supplier or government gives all its tenants or customers on the same terms
Events arranged with a creditor to trigger CDS or this market
Statements or deals not approved by the Group Company's board or authorized officers, except creditors' petitions, missed payments, government or regulator write-downs and default ratings, which count regardless
If the Listed Company merges into another company and ceases to exist (not by name change, reincorporation or new holding company), this market resolves "No" at closing, once pending payment windows and court cases settle, unless a Credit Event already happened.
Timing (all times ET). Dates: bankruptcy at filing (creditors' petitions per item 1); missed payment at the due date when unpaid Obligations first total $10 million, counting only amounts due after this market was created; covenant breach at first public disclosure; restructuring or write-down when it takes effect (it doesn't count if withdrawn or never effective); refusal at the statement, if public within 30 days after December 31, 2027; default rating at the deal or payment it covers if published within 30 days of it, else at publication. Events dated before this market was created don't count, even if completed, confirmed or announced later.
"Yes" resolves once a Credit Event dated from creation through December 31, 2027 is confirmed. "No" resolves 30 days after December 31, 2027, unless an item 2 deadline (including grace periods and outage extensions), an item 3 window, or a publicly filed court or arbitration case deciding whether such an event occurred is still open; then it resolves once they settle, within 90 days after December 31, 2027 at the latest, and anything unconfirmed by then doesn't count. Only the first ruling on the merits counts (for creditors' petitions, the grant); appeals and anything confirmed after resolution don't change the result.
Resolution sources: court records; SEC filings by Group Companies or their counterparties; rating agency publications; trustee notices; FINRA TRACE; US Treasury yield data; and credible reporting.
Any person who possesses material nonpublic information about the contract or its settlement source, or who can influence the outcome, is prohibited from trading it.
