This market will resolve according to the Section 232 duty rate applicable to Canadian unwrought aluminum as of 12:01 AM ET on December 31, 2027.

“Canadian unwrought aluminum” refers to unwrought aluminum not alloyed (HTSUS subheading 7601.10), that is a product of Canada and for which Canada is both the country of smelt and the country of most recent cast.

Only the rate applicable generally to Canadian unwrought aluminum will be considered. Company-specific or project-specific reduced rates, product exclusions, and special rates for US-origin metal will not be considered. Ordinary customs duties, Section 338 duties, antidumping and countervailing duties, and any other tariff will not be considered. If a tariff-rate quota applies, the in-quota rate will be used, whether or not the quota has been filled at the measurement time. If different general rates apply to different goods within subheading 7601.10, the lowest of those rates will be used.

Only the rate legally in effect at the measurement time counts. A change that has been announced or signed but is not in effect as of 12:01 AM ET on December 31, 2027 will not be considered. A change which goes into effect at that time will qualify. A change signed after the measurement time will not be considered, even if it is made retroactive to a date on or before December 31, 2027.

If Section 232 aluminum duties do not apply to Canadian unwrought aluminum at the measurement time for any reason, including an exemption, a termination, a suspension, or a court order that is in effect and has not been stayed, the market will resolve to “0%“.

If the Section 232 duty rate applicable to Canadian unwrought aluminum in effect as of 12:01 AM ET on December 31, 2027 is not expressed solely as an ad valorem percentage, including where it is a specific duty or a combination of a percentage and a specific duty, this market will resolve to “Other.”

The primary resolution sources will be presidential proclamations and executive orders published on whitehouse.gov and in the Federal Register, Federal Register notices issued under them, and HTSUS Chapter 99 as published by the US International Trade Commission. If these sources conflict, the most recent legally effective proclamation, executive order, or Federal Register notice will govern, followed by the HTSUS, and then the US Customs and Border Protection CSMS guidance.
