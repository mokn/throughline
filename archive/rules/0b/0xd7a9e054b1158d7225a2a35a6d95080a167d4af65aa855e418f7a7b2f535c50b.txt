This market will resolve to “Yes” if a diplomatic agreement between the United States and China over AI safety is announced between market creation and the specified date, 11:59 PM ET. Otherwise, this market will resolve to “No”.

A diplomatic agreement refers to an official agreement, treaty, deal, or substantially similar diplomatic instrument that establishes agreed actions, policies, obligations, or commitments between the United States and China.

A diplomatic agreement qualifies as an agreement over AI safety if it is explicitly and unambiguously presented as an AI safety agreement in official announcements from both the United States and China, or if it commits the United States and China to at least one of the following:

i) Mandatory testing of advanced AI models for dangerous capabilities before or after release.
ii) Specific policies to prevent AI from being used to develop biological, chemical, nuclear or cyber weapons or to facilitate attacks using such weapons.
iii) A policy that restricts the use of AI in military weapons, including nuclear weapons, such as a requirement for human control over decisions to use them.
iv) The creation of a dedicated government channel or hotline for AI incidents, or a formal mechanism for sharing information on specific AI risks or incidents. An agreement to hold talks or dialogues on AI alone does not qualify.
v) A policy conditioning the training, release or deployment of AI models on safety requirements.
vi) A cap on the computing power used to train AI models.
vii) A policy limiting the capability level to which AI models or systems may be trained or developed.

Agreements limited to trade, export controls, or topics other than AI safety will not qualify.

Both the United States and China must announce their acceptance of the same qualifying diplomatic agreement for a “Yes” resolution. A joint announcement will qualify, as will separate announcements from each entity of its own acceptance of an agreement which, taken together, directly indicate that all the listed countries accepted the same agreement. Separate announcements of individual policies will not qualify if the policies are not announced as part of a diplomatic agreement.

Each announcement must be a declarative statement that clearly and unambiguously communicates acceptance of an agreement. Statements that reference ongoing negotiations or a prospective agreement, or that allude to or express support for an agreement without confirming acceptance of the agreement, do not qualify. A qualifying announcement need not reference the agreement by name or use specific terminology, provided it clearly communicates acceptance of an agreement.

Whether announcements from the listed countries represent a diplomatic agreement and whether such an agreement qualifies will be primarily determined through the announcements themselves. Where an announcement is made by all listed countries but, based on the announcements, it remains ambiguous whether the announcements represent a qualifying diplomatic agreement between the countries, this market will remain open until either i) definitive confirmation that the announcements represent a qualifying diplomatic agreement between the listed entities is achieved through further announcements from the listed countries or a consensus of credible reporting or ii) 14 calendar days (ET) have passed after the date that the last country made their first potentially qualifying announcement. If, at the end of the fourteenth calendar day, no definitive confirmation has been achieved, whether the agreement qualifies will be adjudicated based on the totality of information available from the resolution sources at that time. No single statement, denial, or presentation of evidence will govern where it is contradicted by the totality of information.

The resolution sources for this market will be official information from the governments of the United States and China and a consensus of credible reporting.
