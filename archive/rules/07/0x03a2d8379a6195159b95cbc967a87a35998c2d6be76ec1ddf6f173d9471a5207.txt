This market will resolve to "Yes" if S&P Global Ratings, Moody's Ratings, or Fitch Ratings publishes a rating action lowering its long-term credit rating for the company named in this market's question by one or more notches between market creation and December 31, 2027, 11:59 PM ET. Otherwise, this market will resolve to "No".

A qualifying downgrade by any one of the three agencies is enough for a "Yes" resolution.

For this market, "long-term credit rating" means S&P Global Ratings' long-term issuer credit rating, Moody's Ratings' long-term issuer rating or Corporate Family Rating (or, where Moody's Ratings has neither, its senior unsecured rating on the company), or Fitch Ratings' Long-Term Issuer Default Rating on the company named in this market's question. A "notch" is one step on the agency's rating scale (for example, AA to AA- on the S&P Global Ratings or Fitch Ratings scale, or Aa2 to Aa3 on the Moody's Ratings scale). Global-scale ratings count, in foreign or local currency; national-scale ratings do not. If Moody's Ratings replaces its issuer rating with a lower Corporate Family Rating in the same action, that counts as a downgrade. A move to a default designation (S&P Global Ratings 'SD' or 'D', or Fitch Ratings 'RD' or 'D') also counts as a downgrade.

The following will not qualify: a change in outlook or CreditWatch/review status alone (for example, Stable to Negative outlook, or placement on review for downgrade); an affirmation, a withdrawal, or a change to a short-term rating; except as set out below, a downgrade of a specific debt issue, a subsidiary, or a financing vehicle that leaves the issuer-level rating unchanged; and rating actions by any agency other than S&P Global Ratings, Moody's Ratings, and Fitch Ratings, including Egan-Jones Ratings, Morningstar DBRS, and Scope Ratings.

If an agency does not rate that company at market creation, only rating actions by the agencies that do rate it can resolve this market.

The date of a rating action is the date the agency first publishes it, not any later effective date. Rating actions published before market creation do not count.

If that company is acquired or merges into another company and its ratings are withdrawn, this market will resolve "No" unless a qualifying downgrade was published before the withdrawal or in the same rating action as the withdrawal.

For this market, "the company" is the parent company named in this market's question (for example, NVIDIA Corporation for "Will NVIDIA (NVDA) get a credit rating downgrade before 2028?"), not any of its subsidiaries.

If, at market creation, the company named in this market's question has no long-term credit rating (as defined above) from S&P Global Ratings, Moody's Ratings or Fitch Ratings, downgrades of its subsidiaries can resolve this market, notwithstanding the exclusion of subsidiary downgrades above.

A qualifying subsidiary is any wholly owned subsidiary of that company that issues debt rated by one of these agencies, including any such subsidiary first rated after market creation.

A downgrade by one or more notches of a qualifying subsidiary's long-term credit rating counts. If the subsidiary has no issuer-level rating, a downgrade of the rating on its senior secured notes counts instead. Other ratings on specific debt issues do not count.

The following subsidiaries qualify in any case: WULF Compute LLC (TeraWulf); APLD ComputeCo LLC, APLD ComputeCo 2 LLC and APLD ComputeCo 3 LLC (Applied Digital); and Core Scientific Finance I LLC (Core Scientific).

The resolution source for this market will be a consensus of credible reporting, including announcements published by the rating agencies themselves.
